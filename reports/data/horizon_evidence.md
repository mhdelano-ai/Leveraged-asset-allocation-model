# Why the target is measured from 21-day returns: evidence from the assembled panel

Generated by `python -m volmodel.data.assemble` from `panel_daily` (re-run it when assets are added). Log EXCESS returns (asset minus cash); a NaN day contributes 0 to the asset (its move is in the next return) while cash still accrues. Overlapping k-day sums use only windows that lie entirely inside one source segment (for pairs: inside one era in which neither asset changes source). Vol = sqrt(mean(s_k²)·252/k) and correlation = Σ s_a s_b / sqrt(Σ s_a² Σ s_b²), both uncentered as in the target definition.

## Summary tables

## What the tables show

**1. One-day vol depends on the pricing convention.** Across segments vol_1/vol_21 runs from 0.69 to 1.20. Low: ust_int H.15 10y CMT par-bond l… (1962-1971) 0.69; hy_credit yahoo_VWEHX (1985-2007) 0.70; ig_credit blend_VWESX_FBNDX (1980-1984) 0.70; reits yahoo:FRESX adjclose (F… (1986-1996) 0.71 (fund NAVs, quoted-yield reconstructions, foreign-close indices: smoothed or stale, positive autocorrelation). High: reits yahoo:VNQ adjclose (Van… (2004-2026) 1.20; em yahoo_EEM (2003-2026) 1.19; us_large yahoo_SPY (1993-2026) 1.15; intl_dev yahoo_EFA (2001-2026) 1.14 (US-listed ETFs: overshoot and reversal, negative autocorrelation). Within one asset the ratio changes at a splice by up to reits 0.49, em 0.42, us_small 0.36, ust_int 0.35: a daily-RV history spliced across these sources has artificial level shifts of 20-50%.

**2. Where the jumps stop.** The share of segments whose k-day vol differs from their 21-day vol by more than two iid standard errors is 67% at k=1, 50% at k=2, 53% at k=3, 43% at k=5, 30% at k=10; the median within-asset spread of r_k falls from 0.31 (k=1) to 0.19 (k=3), 0.14 (k=5) and 0.07 (k=10, max 0.11). So the convention effect is large at 1-3 days, still clearly present at 5, and mostly but not entirely gone at 10. The segments still significant at k=10 (9; latest one ends 2007) are us_small french_me_vw_qnt2_qnt3_… (1926-2000) r10=0.92, ac1=+0.24; hy_credit yahoo_VWEHX (1985-2007) r10=0.93, ac1=+0.15; gold bundesbank:BBEX3 Frankf… (1971-1998) r10=0.94, ac1=-0.02; us_large french_me_vw_hi30 (1926-1993) r10=0.97, ac1=+0.09; ust_int GSW fitted curve, par-b… (1971-2002) r10=0.95, ac1=+0.11; reits yahoo:FRESX adjclose (F… (1986-1996) r10=0.92, ac1=+0.20; ust_int H.15 10y CMT par-bond l… (1962-1971) r10=0.92, ac1=+0.29; intl_dev aqr_mkt_global_ex_usa_p… (1986-2001) r10=0.95, ac1=+0.14; em yahoo_VEIEX (1994-2003) r10=0.93, ac1=+0.22. Beyond 21 days, r_63 is significantly different from 1 in 13% of segments (median 1.01, range 0.84-1.16). The splice table (J_k) tells the same story more noisily; note the placebo splice in commodities (same index both sides) has J values as far from 1 as real splices, so single-splice J values should not be over-read.

**3. Correlation bias.** Relative to 63-day correlations, the median gap is -0.06 at k=1, -0.03 at k=3, -0.02 at k=10 and -0.01 at k=21 (median absolute gap 0.06 / 0.05 / 0.03; most negative -0.44 / -0.28 / -0.13). The iid standard error of c_k − c_63 over a 10-year era is ≈0.07-0.10, so typical gaps are within noise, but the stale-price pairs are not: at k=3 the worst are ig_credit–hy_credit [yahoo_LQD/yahoo_VWEHX 2002-2007] 0.37 vs 0.81; us_large–hy_credit [yahoo_SPY/yahoo_VWEHX 1993-2007] 0.26 vs 0.63; us_large–hy_credit [french_me_vw_…/yahoo_VWEHX 1985-1993] 0.28 vs 0.61; hy_credit–reits [yahoo_VWEHX/yahoo:FRESX a… 1986-1996] 0.37 vs 0.67. Three-day sums (the earlier project convention) remove only part of that bias.

**4. Statistical cost.** The relative standard error of a 12-month realized vol is 4.4% from 1-day returns, 6.5% at k=3, 8.3% at k=5, 11.7% at k=10, 17.1% at k=21 and 31.0% at k=63 (iid normal; fat tails raise the short-k numbers most). A 12-month correlation of 0.5 has a standard error of 0.05 at k=1, 0.13 at k=10 and 0.18 at k=21. The 21-day target is about 3.8x noisier than a daily-RV target: a real loss of power for ranking forecasts, but unbiased (small-sample bias -1.5% on vol at k=21).

## Does anything contradict the choice of 21?

Nothing argues for a SHORTER horizon: at k ≤ 5 the convention effects are large and pervasive, and they are what the target is designed to remove. k=10 removes most of them at 12% instead of 17% standard error, which would be a defensible trade only if the smoothed eras below were handled separately.

Some evidence says 21 days is NOT ALWAYS LONG ENOUGH:

- 63-day vol differs significantly from 21-day vol in: us_small french_me_vw_qnt2_qnt3_… (1926-2000) r63=1.11 (z +4.8); hy_credit yahoo_VWEHX (1985-2007) r63=1.16 (z +3.8); commodities ft:BCOM (excess return)… (1991-2009) r63=1.11 (z +2.3); us_large french_me_vw_hi30 (1926-1993) r63=1.05 (z +2.0). Values above 1 mean variance keeps building beyond a month (smoothing that lasts longer than a month, as the credit report documents for VWEHX, or persistent trends); values below 1 mean mean-reversion over months. The 21-day target is still the right quantity for `cov_rv`, but for the 12-month variance the bridge (`volmodel.bridge`) must carry era-specific variance ratios for these segments.
- 21-day correlations still differ significantly from 63-day ones in: intl_dev–ust_long [yahoo_EFA/TLT adjclose … 2002-2026] -0.11 vs -0.21 (z +2.2). Whether such a gap is a measurement artefact or a genuine horizon effect (stock-bond correlation, for instance, is known to differ between monthly and quarterly horizons) has to be judged pair by pair.
- The largest 21-day gaps in absolute terms (not all significant, because the eras are short) are ig_credit–hy_credit [yahoo_LQD/yahoo_VWEHX 2002-2007] 0.68 vs 0.81; hy_credit–reits [yahoo_VWEHX/yahoo:VGSIX a… 1996-2004] 0.42 vs 0.53; us_large–hy_credit [yahoo_SPY/yahoo_VWEHX 1993-2007] 0.52 vs 0.63. Where one side is a smoothed NAV (VWEHX for high yield before 2007-04 in particular), correlations are attenuated even at 21 days; models fitted on those eras will understate co-movement unless they correct for it.
- The z-scores assume iid returns; with volatility clustering the true noise is larger, so the lists above are, if anything, too long rather than too short.
