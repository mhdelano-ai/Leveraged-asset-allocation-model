Allocation Inputs Study

Forecast, history, or neither: what should set the allocation?

Stocks beside a sleeve of intermediate Treasuries, gold and managed futures. Eighteen weighting rules, each fixed in advance, replayed month by month from 1990 with only what was known at the time. Returns through 2026-10-31.

Descriptive allocation study; it replays weighting rules on a history that was all seen before the rules were written, selects nothing and is not out-of-sample evidence.

Expected returns
Do not forecast them, and do not optimize on them

A holding's return in one five-year block said nothing about the next (correlation +0.01 over 47 pairs of blocks). Gold's return per unit of risk was -0.18 in the first half of the window and +0.45 in the second; managed futures went from +1.28 to +0.22. Since the live funds began, all three optimizers trailed a third each (best of them -0.09 in Sharpe ratio, none clearly different). Over all four holdings, the optimizer that did best over the whole replay (optimized: past returns, forecast risk) had a Sharpe ratio +0.36 against the fixed 60/40 mix's (better), at 5.6% volatility against 9.5%; since the live funds began, -0.09 (no clear difference). Before 2007 the replay's managed futures are the paper factor.

Weights inside the sleeve
Size by long-run risk, or simply a third each

Risk does carry over: a block's volatility against the next, correlation +0.60. Weighting by 1/volatility on the whole past ran at 5.7% volatility against 6.8% for a third each, with a worst fall of -9% against -14%. Its Sharpe ratio was +0.10 higher over the whole replay (better) and -0.01 since the live funds began (no clear difference). The forecast gave the same weights for 12 times the trading.

How much risk in total
Your choice; use the forecast to know what you chose

No input picks the stock share: it is the risk you accept. Scaling the 60/40 mix to a 10% risk target with the forecast gave about the same Sharpe ratio as leaving it fixed (0.56 against 0.57, no clear difference) with a worst fall of -25% against -32%; scaling with past volatility made no clear difference (-0.00). The forecast's 12-month matrix stood +23% above month-to-month volatility, so a target set with it runs below the target.

What the covariance forecast adds

Each source's reading of the next 12 months' volatility, scored at every month-end. Loss is QLIKE, lower is better. The forecast tracks changes in risk, which the two historical windows do not, but its level stands above what month-to-month returns then showed.

The forecast does not cover managed futures. The 21-day row was added after the first run and no rule uses it.

What this does not show

  • Every year in the replay had been seen before the rules were written, and the three sleeve assets were chosen on the same years.
  • Managed futures before 2007-03 are a paper factor scaled to the live funds' volatility, less a gap measured on 2007 to 2026. The live-funds period is the one to trust, and it is short.
  • No trading costs, fund fees or taxes, and month-end values miss falls inside a month.
  • Most differences between rules are small beside their intervals.
  • The scaled rules were rerun once after the first results, with exposure capped at 100% of capital instead of twice capital, when the package dropped borrowing.
