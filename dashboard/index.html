How to read these numbers, and what they cannot tell you
  • The model is an average: nine families of volatility models are averaged within each family and then across families, dropping the highest and lowest for each asset; 25 correlation forecasts are averaged; and the result is scaled up to the 12-month horizon, because 12-month returns are more dispersed than short-horizon volatility implies.
  • Short-horizon volatility is measured from 21-day returns, not daily ones. Daily volatility depends on how a fund or index is priced and is not comparable across sources or eras.
  • The forecast gives the expected level of risk over a year. It does not say when a crisis will start: high-yield bonds realized about three times their forecast in the year covering March 2020.
  • If the covariance matrix is fed to an optimizer, the risk of the optimized portfolio was under-predicted by about 1.4 times before 2014. Scale it up, or constrain the weights.
  • The backtest can only detect large differences between models. Twelve held-out years can show that nothing collapsed, not which model is best.
  • Data: US equities daily from 1926, Treasuries from 1962, gold from 1971, other assets later; all from free public sources and audited independently. The numbers on this page are a fixed snapshot made on 14 November 2026.
