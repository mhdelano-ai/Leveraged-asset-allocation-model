    How the point is picked

    1. One mix: the four holdings weighted by 1/volatility on the whole past.
    2. Scale it to the goal's drawdown budget: the largest multiple of capital whose worst fall in the replay stayed within the budget. Below 100% the rest is T-bills. Above 100% it is carried inside stacked funds, or on a margin loan in a taxable account that chooses one.
    3. If the account can carry no more before the budget is used, stocks take the place of diversifiers at that limit, up to 100% stocks. Stacked funds reach 1.15 times capital with this mix and a margin loan 2 times.
    4. Money needed within 3 years has no budget and stays in T-bills.

    What this does not show

    • The drawdown budgets are judgements, and the answers follow from them. The table above shows what any other budget would give.
    • Every goal beyond 3 years ends where the account can carry no more: the mix scaled that far fell only -10% with stacked funds and -18% on margin. So in practice the budget sets the stock share and the diversifiers fill whatever the account can hold.
    • On margin that limit is Regulation T's for opening a position, 2 times capital. The broker's maintenance requirement is not modelled, and it decides how far the position can fall before it is called.
    • On margin the lower end of expected growth falls as the mix is levered, from +0.8% at 100% of capital to 0.0% at 2 times. Whether the diversifiers earn more than the loan costs is not established.
    • Every year in the replay had been seen before the rule was written, the diversifiers were chosen on the same years, and managed futures before 2007-03 are a paper factor. A worst fall in the past does not bound the next one.
    • Month-end values miss falls inside a month. Taxes, trading costs and fund fees are not charged, so tax-deferred and Roth accounts get the same numbers.
    • This is arithmetic on the package's studies, not advice.
