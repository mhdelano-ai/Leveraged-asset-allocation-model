Return stacking or a margin loan: who does the borrowing

Both put more than 100% of your capital to work. With return stacking the fund borrows, inside itself, and you only own the fund. With margin you borrow, from the broker, and the loan sits on your account.

Return-stacked funds

  • You buy funds that each hold stocks and add a diversifier on top with futures.
  • The borrowing happens inside the fund, at close to the T-bill rate. You owe nothing, so there is no loan on your account for anyone to call, and the most you can lose is what you put in.
  • The price is higher fund fees and less choice: each fund fixes its own blend, and the stocks inside are US or global stocks.

Margin loan

  • You buy plain funds, then borrow from the broker against them to buy more.
  • The loan costs T-bills + 1.38%. If the account falls far enough, the broker can demand cash or sell your holdings at the bottom.
  • You choose every fund and the loan reaches 2 times capital, but it is not allowed in a retirement account.

This goal holds T-bills, so nothing is held above 100% of capital and the choice does not arise.

One way to hold it

    Funds and fees are a hand-recorded lookup from 2026-11-16, using the cheapest recorded funds that fit. Confirm on the issuer's page. No stock fund's fee is recorded.

    The two dials

    1. Stocks: the largest share, in steps of 5%, whose worst fall in the replay stayed within the goal's drawdown budget. The fall is measured on the whole portfolio, diversifiers included.
    2. Diversifiers: as much as the account can carry beside those stocks, always 44% Treasuries, 19% gold, 37% managed futures. Stacked funds carry them inside, with no loan. A margin loan carries 2 times capital in all.
    3. Money needed within 3 years has no budget and stays in T-bills.

    What this does not show

    • The drawdown budgets are judgements, and the stock shares follow from them. The table above shows what any other budget would give.
    • The diversifiers fall too. With no stocks at all, 100% of capital in them fell -9% in the replay, and 200% on margin fell -21%.
    • A margin loan is 100% of capital at every goal. That is Regulation T's limit for opening a position. The broker's maintenance requirement is not modelled, and it decides how far the position can fall before it is called. Whether the diversifiers earn more than the loan costs is not established.
    • Every year in the replay had been seen before the rule was written, the diversifiers were chosen on the same years, and managed futures before 2007-03 are a paper factor. A worst fall in the past does not bound the next one.
    • Month-end values miss falls inside a month. Taxes, trading costs and fund fees are not charged, so tax-deferred and Roth accounts get the same numbers.
    • This is arithmetic on the package's studies, not advice.
